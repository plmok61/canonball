# Brainstorm Prompt — What Happens When the Ship Arrives

Paste this at the start of a fresh session.

---

I'm writing a short story and I've hit a fork in the road. The canon files for this project are in `canon/` — please read all four before we start (`themes.md`, `world.md`, `characters.md`, `timeline.md`). Also read any files in `notes/` that exist.

Here's the setup so you have the full picture:

**The story:** A robot is alone on a planet that is entirely covered in trash. We don't know yet whether this is Earth in the distant future, an alien world used as a dump, or something else — that's one of the things we're figuring out. The robot has one job: carry loads of trash to a compactor on the far side of a ridge, one trip at a time, and compress it. It does this every day. It has been doing this for a very long time.

**The robot:** It has no memory of being built or deployed. It doesn't know if it was given a purpose or developed one. It doesn't speak. It doesn't ask questions. It works. Whether there is anything happening inside — longing, satisfaction, habit, something that resembles peace — is the story's central unresolved question. We should resist answering it too cleanly.

**What has happened so far:** One day, a ship appears in the sky. It doesn't land. It just hangs there — visible, unignorable. This is the inciting moment. Everything before the ship is prologue. Everything after is the story.

**What the story must not do:** Explain too much. The ship's origin, the robot's history, the fate of whoever made all this trash — these should stay partially or fully unanswered. The story is not a mystery to be solved. It is a mood to be inhabited.

**What I need from this session:** Help me brainstorm what happens next. Push me to make choices. Don't resolve things prematurely — your job is to open doors, not close them. Offer multiple directions and argue for the ones you find most interesting. Ask me questions that force me to commit to things I've been avoiding.

Specific things to figure out:

- What does the robot do when it first sees the ship? Does it stop? Does it keep working? Does it hide?
- Does the ship eventually land, send something down, broadcast a signal — or does it just leave?
- Is there a someone on the ship, and do they matter as a character — or is the ship more like a weather event, something that happens to the robot rather than someone who acts on it?
- What does the robot want, if it wants anything? Does the ship change that?
- What is the emotional register of the ending — something like grief, or relief, or neither?
- Should we ever leave the robot's perspective, even briefly?
- How long is the timeline of the story — hours, days, years?
