# World

## The Planet

No name. Possibly no atmosphere capable of supporting biological life — or possibly just barely capable; this is unresolved. The sky is a pale, washed-out color that could be overcast or could simply be how the sky looks here. There is no weather to speak of, or weather so constant it has become background.

The surface is covered in trash as far as the robot can see. Not chaos — it has been here long enough that the trash has settled into a kind of landscape. There are hills of it, valleys of it. Things rust and compress and partially decompose and become topography.

There is no other structure visible except:

**The Compactor** — a large industrial machine built into the base of a ridge. It still functions. It has always functioned. The robot feeds loads into it; it compresses them; a cube of compacted material is expelled out the back and added to a growing stack. The stack is very large.

**The Ridge** — the natural (or trash-made) elevation the robot crosses on each trip. From the top, on a clear moment, you can see a long way. This is where the robot first sees the ship.

## Scale and Distance

One trip — from the robot's current working area to the compactor and back — takes approximately four hours. The robot makes several trips per day. The working area shifts slowly as the robot clears sections and moves to adjacent ones. Progress is real but imperceptible at any human timescale.

## The Ship

Appears one day in the upper atmosphere. Large enough to be clearly visible from the surface — not a point of light, but a shape. It does not immediately descend. It does not immediately leave. Its design is unspecified; it should not look like anything familiar.
