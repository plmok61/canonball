# Characters

## The Robot

### Role
The sole inhabitant of the planet. Carrier of trash. The story's only consistent point of view.

### Biography
Unknown. It has no memory of being built, delivered, or assigned its task. Its serial number is partly worn away. It may have been working for decades. It may have been working for centuries. It does not know.

### Appearance
Roughly humanoid — two arms, two legs, a torso — but built for load-bearing, not appearance. Its chassis is dented and discolored from years of exposure. One knee joint clicks on the uphill stretch to the compactor. It has never been repaired.

### Psychology
Unknowable, by design. It does not speak or signal. It completes a trip, turns around, begins the next. Whether this constitutes purpose, habit, or something resembling contentment is the story's central unresolved question. Do not resolve it.

### Motivation
Surface: carry trash to the compactor. Deeper: unknown. The question of whether those two things are different is what the story is asking.

### Arc
Unchanged, or changed entirely — depending on how the ship is handled. The robot ends the story doing the same thing it was doing at the start. Whether that means something different by then is up to the reader.

---

## The Ship (not a character, but treated like one)

The ship does not land at first. It hangs in the sky like a second sun. It may contain people. It may not. Its intentions are never stated. It functions more like weather than character — something that arrives and changes the atmosphere without explaining itself.

If someone from the ship ever makes contact, they should remain partial: a voice, a silhouette, a hand. Never a full person with a name and a backstory.
