# Themes

## Core Philosophical Question
Does work have meaning when there is no one to witness it — and does the answer change when someone finally appears?

This must never be stated explicitly in the prose. Only shown.

---

## Primary Themes

### 1. Purpose Without Audience
The robot has been working in total solitude. Its labor is real — the trash moves, the compactor runs — but there is no one to receive it, no one to benefit, no measure of progress. The story asks whether purpose requires an observer, or whether it can exist as pure function.

### 2. The Unknowability of Inner Life
We cannot enter the robot's mind with certainty. The prose should stay at the surface — physical sensation, observable behavior — and let the reader project. Interiority should be implied, never confirmed.

### 3. Disruption of the Sufficient
Before the ship, the robot's world was complete in its smallness. The ship introduces the possibility of something else — rescue, context, company, destruction. The story is about what happens to a life that was sufficient when the insufficient is offered.

---

## What the Story Must Never Do

- Never confirm whether the robot is conscious, suffering, or at peace. Ambiguity is the point.
- Never explain where the trash came from or what happened to whoever made it.
- Never make the ship's arrival straightforwardly good or bad — resist the rescue narrative and the threat narrative equally.
- Never give the robot a name. It is "the robot" throughout.
- Never end on resolution. The final image should be open.
